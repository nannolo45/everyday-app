Everything you need to set Everyday up and plan your days with it.

What Everyday is

Everyday is a planner for your days. There are two kinds of things in it: events, which already have a date (a concert, a visit, a class), and tasks, which still need one — you write them down and the app helps you decide when to do them. Every task takes some time, and once you put it on a day it becomes a block on that day's timeline, next to your events and calendar appointments. Tasks can also be tied to an event ("buy the tickets, two weeks before"), so the event carries its own to-do list. The app can suggest a plan for a day, a week or a month, keep Google Calendar in step, add the time it takes to get to a place, and plan meals and grocery runs together with the Ricette recipe app.

It works in the browser on a phone or a computer and can be installed like a normal app. Your data stays yours: it lives on your devices and in a private space on GitHub that only you can see. There is no account with us, no AI and nothing to pay.

What it is not: it is not a calendar (your appointments come from Google Calendar), not a notes app and not a tool for a team. One person, one set of data.

First setup

You need about fifteen minutes and a GitHub account (free). Everything happens in Settings: tap the gear at the top right. Settings is a short list, and each item opens a page of its own: My day, Travel, Categories, Shops, Advanced (the connections and everything you set once), Language and the Guide. The card at the top says whether your data is in sync, with Sync now (or Connect, before you have).

1. Install the app

Open the app's address in Chrome on your phone, or in any browser on a computer. On Android choose Install app (or Add to Home screen) from the browser menu; on a computer click the small install icon in the address bar. The app works without a connection too; new versions arrive on their own, and a bar offers to reload. The bottom of Settings shows which version you have.

2. Connect your own space on GitHub

This is where your data is stored and how your devices stay in sync.

  1. On GitHub, create a private, empty repository (a "repository" is just a folder on GitHub), for example everyday-data. Don't copy anyone else's.
  2. Create a personal access token (GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens) that is allowed to read and write the Contents of that repository. A token is like a password made for apps.
  3. In Settings → Advanced → Connections → GitHub sync (or tap Connect on the card at the top of Settings), type your GitHub user name, the repository name and the token, then tap Save and test. The badge at the top of the app says Synced when it works.

The token stays on the device. Do step 3 on every phone or computer you use: they all share the same data, within a minute of any change.

3. Optional: Ricette (recipes and pantry)

If you also use the Ricette app, type the name of its data repository in Settings → Advanced → Ricette (the same token must be allowed to use it). Everyday then knows your recipes and your pantry, shares the shopping list with Ricette and can plan meals from recipes.

4. Optional: Google Calendar

This link runs on GitHub, not on your phone, so it needs a one-time setup on Google's side. Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar lists the steps; in short, you create a small Google Cloud project, allow it to use your calendar, and store three "secrets" in your repository. Once done, your appointments appear in the app and your plan appears in Google Calendar, with reminders.

5. Travel

In Settings → Travel, type your home address and how you usually get around (walking, bike, public transport, car). That's all the app needs to add travel time to tasks that have a place. A Google Maps key is optional; without one the app uses free map services.

6. A second person

Each person needs their own repository, their own token and (if they want it) their own calendar setup. On their device, open the same app address and type their repository in Settings → Advanced → Connections. If the device held someone else's data before, the app asks whether to start empty — choose that. Empty this device, at the bottom of the same page, does the same at any later time.

The four tabs

Tab What it is for
Today Home. The next move with its leave and start times, today's list, what is pressing, the week at a glance. Tapping the app's name brings you back here.
Events Everything with a fixed date, nearest first: your own events and the ones from Google Calendar, each with how ready it is.
Tasks Three lists with the same layout: To do (to plan, planned, done), Routine (the things that come back: chores, the school run, the gym, meals) and Shopping. Each has a search field and one ⇅ button for sorting and filters.
Planner Your plan as a Day, a Week or a Month, with a drawer of the tasks still to plan and the Plan button that lays them out for you.

The round + at the bottom right adds something from Today, Events and Tasks; see Adding things. The order of the tabs is the order you plan in: see what's on today, put the dated things in, add the tasks around them, then let the Planner lay everything out. The gear at the top right opens Settings. On a phone, swipe left or right to move between the tabs. The badge next to the gear shows whether your data is in sync: Synced, Syncing, Offline, or Only on this device when you haven't connected yet.

Today

The next move card answers one question: when do I have to move? For a task with a place it shows two times — leave 11:00 → start 11:20 — and counts down to the leave time. On my way ticks the trip, Done finishes the task, the pin opens the address in Maps, Move opens the block. Below it, today's list shows every block and appointment in order (an orange time means "leave then"), and a line tells you how much free time you have until the next thing — tap it to see what fits.

Pressing lists what needs you: overdue tasks, tasks due in the next days that have no day yet, and tasks missing a detail. Today puts one in the next free slot; Fix opens it on the missing detail. The week strip at the bottom shows how full each day is; tap a day to open it in the Planner.

To fix this week. For a repeating task whose time you pick each week (see Recurring tasks), a card at the top of Today offers free slots for each day it's due: Run · Tomorrow with 17:30, 19:15, 09:00 or Other…. Tap a time to put it there, Skip to leave that day out, or Fix the highlighted ones to take the first offer for all of them. The card for the week ahead appears on Sunday at 18:00; you can move that to another day or time in Settings → My day → Card "To fix this week". Today's occurrences always show. On the weekly card, Review the week opens Routine on the week ahead, so you can see everything at once: what's in place, what's still to fix, what to skip or pause.

Every morning, the first time you open Today, what was left undone on earlier days goes back on the plan over the coming week; a line says how many things moved, with Undo.

A task that takes more than one day. If a day ends with only some of a task's steps ticked — say 3 of 8 — that day isn't moved: it stays on the plan as a trace, striped, with ◐ 3/8 and the steps you ticked. Only the 5 steps left are planned again, as a new block marked day 2 (then day 3, and so on). So looking back, you see every day the task was on your plate and what you did on each. A day where you didn't get to it at all simply moves, as before. Open the task and it says ◐ 3 of 8 steps done · on your plan since Mon 26 Oct · 3 days, with the done steps marked ✓.

Doing a task: the timer

▶ Start now is in every task and in every block of today. The block moves to now (or a new one starts now), and a bar at the top of the app shows the task and the time running: Water the plants · since 08:30 · planned 10m · 0:07. Pause stops the clock while you're interrupted; Done ends it. The block then keeps the real time it took, and the task remembers how long it usually takes you (Real time · usually 25m in the task), which the planner uses next time. One timer runs at a time; starting another ends the first.

If you start late and the task now runs into what comes next, Today says so and offers Move the next ones to the planner's next good slots (with Undo). If you finish early, it offers Move up for the next task of the day, into the time you freed.

Events

An event is something with a date you don't choose: a concert on the 20th of May, the dentist on Thursday at 9, Luca's wedding in June. You put it in as soon as you hear about it, and from then on it is on the plan, in Google Calendar, and on Today when the day comes. The planner never moves an event.

Adding an event

Type it in the field at the top of the Events tab, or tap + there (on the other tabs, tap Event in the sheet): Concert 20 May 21:00 @Arena 3h, Dentist 12/11 at 9, Luca's wedding 12 Jun, Pottery class thu 18:30 2h. The app reads the date (20 May, 20/5, fri, tomorrow), the time (21:00, at 9), the place (@…) and the length (2h). Without a time it is an all-day event. Without a date the editor opens and asks for one — a date is the only thing an event needs.

Tap an event to open it. It is the same editor as a task, with the same parts in the same order; only the first one differs:

Part What you set there
When The date (required) and the time (leave it empty for all day); repetition for a weekly class; the reminder.
To do before and after The tasks tied to this event, with their lead time and whether each is planned — see below.
Length and category How long it takes (two hours until you say otherwise), a buffer after it, the category.
Place, Steps, Checklist, Notes Exactly as for a task: a place adds Get there with the real travel time, so Today shows leave 20:15 → start 21:00.

Tasks tied to an event

This is what makes events useful. In the event, + To do before / after adds a task with a lead time: Buy the tickets · 2 weeks before, Book the train · 1 week before, Thank Anna · 1 day after. Each one is a normal task: it appears in the Tasks tab (marked 📅 with the event's name), gets its due date from the event, shows up in Pressing when it slips, and the Planner places it like anything else. Move the event and all its tasks move with it.

Inside the event you see at a glance where you stand — 3 to do · 2 to plan, then ✓ all done — and Plan these lays the open ones out over the days before the event. The same badge shows in the Events list and on Today's next-move card. Open a tied task and its When part says For the event "Concert" · 2 weeks before; change the lead time there, or untie it.

Events imported from Google Calendar can take tied tasks too: open one from the Events list and add them the same way. The event itself stays Google's (you change it there), but its preparation lives here.

Events on the plan

On the Day timeline an event is a block with a 📅 and a stronger colour. Tapping an empty spot on the timeline makes a new event at that time (you picked a time, so it is fixed); + Add → Event does the same. You can still drag an event's block by hand; the planner, the morning re-flow and Plan leave events alone and work around them. Events go to Google Calendar like everything else, all-day ones as all-day entries. An event with a time is always one block: change it after the fact and that block changes; delete the event and it leaves the plan, past days included.

Tasks

Adding things

Tap the round +. A sheet opens with one field, already set for the tab you're on: a task on Today and To do, a routine on Routine, an item on Shopping, an event on Events. The chips at the top switch it: Task · Event · Routine · Meal · Shopping. What you've typed stays when you switch.

A few shortcuts save you from opening the task: Dentist 1h sets how long it takes, !1 to !5 how important it is (1 = very), #work a category (#work #home for two), @Via Roma 12 the place, fri or tomorrow the day, by fri a due date, every mon a weekly repeat. Under the field, chips show what the app understood: 20m · Work · due Fri.

For a task there are three ways to finish:

  • Add and plan puts it in the first free slot right away; the button says where (first free slot: Wed 09:00). With a day and a time (Call Marco tue 15:00) it goes exactly there.
  • Just add puts it on the list, to plan later. Pressing Enter does the same and keeps the sheet open for the next one.
  • Choose when… opens the free slots of the coming days to pick from.

More details (steps, place, rules) opens the full editor. A new task shows on top of its list under Just added, and a bar says where it went.

On Shopping and Events, Enter adds the line and keeps the sheet open, listing what you've added so far. Meal opens the meal editor.

Hold the + (or right-click it on a computer) for the full menu: Task, Event, From a routine, Meal and Import a list, which takes a whole list you paste in, one task per line.

Tap a task to open it. The task is split into parts you can fold; anything you don't touch keeps a sensible value.

Part What you set there
Length and importance How long it takes (30 minutes until you say otherwise), a buffer after it (a few spare minutes added to the block), how important it is, one or more categories (the first one gives the colour), and the Routine switch.
When First one choice: Once, Repeats or Whenever (see below); then only what that needs: a day and a time, a due date, the part of the day you prefer, things that must come first (type a few letters to find a task, or make a new one on the spot), time windows (e.g. office hours), the reminder. For a task tied to an event: the event and the lead time instead of a due date.
Place An address or a link. With a place, the app adds Get there (and, if you like, Come back) with the real travel time.
Steps Parts of the task, each with its own length; on the plan every step gets its own time. Tap ⚓ on the step the task's time refers to (e.g. Workout at 19:00, so Get there goes before).
Checklist Things to tick while doing it.
Rules What should happen before or after, depending on how it goes (something to prepare before, a follow-up if it fails, a grocery run before a meal…). Shown when Rules, templates and preparations is on under Settings → Advanced; day templates and preparations are then listed on that page too.
Notes Free text.

Routine

Routine holds what comes back: cleaning, the washing, picking the children up from school, the gym, the run, meals. Some have a fixed rhythm, some come back now and then. You don't need them every day; you look at them once a week and adjust. Every task that repeats shows here, and so does every task you tick Routine on. One-off to-dos don't; neither do the travel legs, which live in Settings → Travel.

The tab is read by the week. On top: Week 2–8 Nov, with how it's going (9 of 12 in place · 2 to fix); ‹ › move to other weeks, and tapping the week brings you back to this one. Below, the routines are grouped by area (their category), then Meals (from Ricette when it's connected), then No rhythm for the ones you plan by hand. Each routine with a rhythm has a row of seven days:

Mark Means
● full done
○ ring on the plan
dashed square to fix: it's due that day, the time isn't picked yet
– dash skipped (or paused)
crossed ring missed: the day passed and it didn't happen

A routine without a rhythm has a ▶ instead, to plan it (pick a day and a slot); + Add → From a routine on the Planner does the same. The + on this tab adds a new routine (Bins out every thu 10m), and + New routine at the bottom does too.

Tap a routine to see how it's been going and change what you change from time to time:

  • Recent weeks: the last three weeks and this one, with 8 of 10 done.
  • When: the rhythm, the time, the length (it opens the When part, see below).
  • Period: from when and until when, e.g. the school run from 14 Sep to 30 Jun. Nothing is planned outside it.
  • Pauses: a stretch of time when it rests (the Christmas holidays, two weeks of illness). Pick the start and 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks or an end date; leave the end empty to pause until you take it off. Whatever was planned in a pause comes off the plan, and it comes back by itself after.
  • Skip a day this week: one tap takes that day off the plan.
  • More details opens the full editor.

Recurring tasks

The When part starts with one choice:

  • Once: a day and a time, or neither (the planner finds a slot), and a due date if there is one.
  • Repeats: Every day, Some days (pick the days, e.g. Tue and Thu), N times a week or Every month, with Every N and an end date if you like. Then And the time?:
  • Always at the same time: every occurrence of the coming weeks goes on the plan by itself (marked ↻).
  • I pick it each week: the days are fixed, the time isn't. The To fix this week card on Today offers free slots for each day (see Today). N times a week always works this way.
  • Whenever: no day; it waits on the list until you plan it (handy for routines without a rhythm).

Change one occurrence from its block, or the whole series from the task; when you change or delete from a block the app asks only this one or this and the following.

Finding things

To do, Routine and Shopping share one toolbar: a search field and a ⇅ button that shows the current sort (Shopping's has views of its own, see Meals, shopping list and grocery runs). The search looks through titles, notes, places, steps and checklists. ⇅ opens Sort and filter: sort by smart, your own order (drag ⠿), due date, importance, length, category or date added; Compact for one line per task; and the filters: To fix (tasks the planner still needs a detail for: a length, a place, your home address…), Recurring, With a place, or one category. Active filters show as chips under the search; tap one to remove it.

Every row reads the same way: the title, one line of details (day, length, place, category), and the importance as orange ! marks on the right. A ⚠ means a detail is missing.

Planning

The views

The Planner shows your plan as a Day (a timeline from the start to the end of your day — you choose the hours in Settings → My day — with a red line for now), a Week (seven columns) or a Month (a grid). Every day has a small busy bar: how full it is compared with the daily limit you set. ‹ › move in time, Today brings you back, and tapping a day in the Week or Month opens it.

The drawer

At the bottom of the Planner sits To plan: the tasks that have no day yet. Open it to see them (search, sort, filter), tap ↓ on a row to put it in the next free slot of the day on screen, or hold and drag it onto the timeline, a week day or a month cell. The same drawer shows the previews when you let the app plan.

Putting a task on a day by hand

  • Tap an empty spot on the Day timeline: a new event at that time.
  • + Add → a Task (new or from the list), an Event, a Routine or a Meal.
  • Drag a block to move it; drag its bottom edge to make it longer or shorter. Tap a block to tick it, see its steps and checklist, or change it.

Letting the app plan

✦ Plan is one guided flow. Pick the span (today, a few days, a week, two weeks, the month). If some tasks are missing a detail the planner needs, it asks right there — a length with one tap, a place, whether to split a task longer than a day's limit — no need to open them. Then the previews appear on the plan, inside your planning hours, respecting due dates, importance, your own order, time windows, things that must come first and the daily limit; each preview says why it landed where it did. Remove the ones you don't want, then Add in the drawer makes them real and the calendar follows. Tasks that are already planned are kept apart, so you don't plan them twice.

If a block overlaps a calendar event, or comes before something it depends on, a warning appears with a Fix button. A task with a ⚠ is missing a detail the planner wants: To fix in the filters lists them, and the Plan flow asks for them.

Travel

Give a task a place and the app adds Get there before it, with the travel time from your home (or from the place of the previous task that day, if you don't go home in between) and the transport you chose. Come back after it is optional. Both are steps of the task: you see them on the block, you can tick them, and the block grows around the task so the task itself stays at its time. Everywhere the block appears — Today, the Planner, Google Calendar — it reads with two times: leave 11:00 · 11:20–12:20.

In Settings → Travel → Journey legs you decide when each one is added: only when the task has a place, for every task (a task without a place then shows a "?" of 30 minutes as a reminder to account for the trip) or never (you switch it on per task). Each task can still say otherwise in its Place part, and pick its own transport.

The legs are tasks of their own. Before the task (getting there) and After the task (coming back) each point to a task — Get there and Come back at first. You edit them from Settings → Travel: rename them, or give them steps — for example Pack the bag (5 min) and Travel. The step marked ⚓ (or the first one) receives the travel time from the map; the others keep their own length. You can also pick any routine for either leg.

Travel times come from free map services. With a Google Maps key in Settings, Google is used instead and public transport works everywhere. A "?" on a task means the time isn't known yet: the planner counts 30 minutes until it is, and ⚠ tells you when an address wasn't found. Start from where I am today uses your phone's location for the first trip of the day.

Meals, shopping list and grocery runs

With Ricette connected, + Add → Meal puts a meal on a day and lets you pick recipes for it; the preparation step takes its length from the recipes. When the time comes (or when you tick the check step) the app looks at the pantry: what's missing goes on the shopping list, and a grocery run is planned before the meal.

The Shopping part of the Tasks tab (To do · Routine · Shopping; a grocery-run block opens it directly) is the same list as in Ricette, both ways: add, change, tick or delete here and it shows there within a minute. It also looks and works the same in both apps. ⓘ next to the search explains this and shows when the list last synced, with a button to sync now.

  • Adding. Tap + and type 2 l milk, dish soap or shampoo; Enter adds each one and keeps the sheet open, so you can type the whole list in one go. Each item lands in its department by itself — and in the same one whatever language you type in (cavolfiore, Blumenkohl and cauliflower all go under Fruit & veg). Household and personal care have departments of their own. Items Ricette adds because something is running low carry a running low label.
  • ⇅ View. The button next to the search shows the current view; tap it to choose Departments, Recipes, Shops, My order, A–Z, Newest first or Oldest first, and to filter: only what you can get at one shop, or only what is running low. Active filters show as chips under the search; tap one to remove it.
  • Ticking. Tick an item at the shop and it leaves its group for the cart at the bottom: In the cart · 3. Tap the count to see what's in it; Put in pantry puts the food into Ricette's pantry (household and care items just leave the list).
  • An item. Tap its name to change the name, the quantity (− / +), the unit, the department, the note and the shops that have it; + New shop adds one right there. Delete is in the same sheet, with Undo.
  • ⋯ More. Plan the shopping (the grocery runs), Share the list, Select (tick several items to give them shops or delete them together), Reorder (drag ⠿ to put departments and items in the order of your supermarket; My order always has the handles) and Empty the cart.

Shops (Settings → Shops) have opening hours and, if you like, an address. Say which shops carry an item and the grocery runs are planned when those shops are open, as one run or one per shop. A grocery run is a block like any other; it lists what to buy there, and ticking items as you buy them marks them bought in Ricette too.

Google Calendar

Once set up, the link works both ways:

  • Your calendars → Everyday. Appointments from the calendars you import appear on the plan in their colour, as busy time the planner works around. You can't change them here; a link opens them in Google Calendar.
  • Everyday → Google Calendar. Everything you plan is written to a calendar called Everyday, with a reminder (the default from Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar, or the task's own). A task with a trip is one event that starts when you leave, titled Dentist 11:20 · leave 11:00, so the reminder rings before you have to go. Your own events carry a 📅; all-day ones become all-day entries. Move or delete an Everyday event in Google Calendar and your plan follows.

The link refreshes every hour and a moment after you change your plan; Sync with Google now refreshes it by hand. The section in Settings shows when it last ran and how many appointments came in. Remove calendar data deletes the imported appointments from your repository — useful if a repository was copied from someone else's and shows their calendar.

A typical week

Sunday evening, ten minutes — left to right across the tabs. Open Events and put in anything with a date you heard about during the week (Dentist thu 9:00 @…, Dinner at Marco's sat 20:00); for the big ones add what has to happen before (+ To do before / after). Then the +: empty your head, with a length when you know it (Call the bank 20m, Tax return 2h !2), and a place for the things that happen somewhere. Put the week's meals on their days (+ Add → Meal) and pick the recipes. If the To fix this week card is up, tap the times for the gym and the runs. Then Planner → ✦ Plan → a week: answer the two or three details it asks, look at the previews, drag the one or two you want elsewhere, Add. Your appointments were already there, so nothing lands on top of them; the grocery run was placed before the first meal that needs it.

Every morning. Open the app: Today shows the next move with its leave time (an event's card also says how ready it is), the day's list, and anything that slipped from yesterday already back on the plan (Undo if you disagree). Google Calendar reminds you before each block. Pressing shows what needs a decision — one tap puts it on today. If the day looks too full, open the Planner and drag something to tomorrow.

During the day. Tap ▶ Start now when you begin something, and Done on the bar when you finish; or just tick blocks as you finish them (for long ones, tick the steps). A meeting ended early? The free-time line on Today says how long you have and what fits. Something new came up? Tap +: call Marco tomorrow 15m, or tap Event for Concert 20 May 21:00. Buy groceries from the Shopping tab, ticking items as they go into the basket.

Evening. What you didn't do stays open: tomorrow morning it is back on the plan, and the next Plan picks up the rest. Overdue tasks sit at the top of Pressing and of To plan, so they don't get lost.

Once in a while. Recurring things (gym Monday and Wednesday at 19:00, weekly cleaning) are set once and appear on their own; for the ones whose time changes, I pick it each week brings them to the Sunday card. Routine shows them all by the week, with pauses for the holidays. Settings → My day is where you adjust the day's hours, the daily limit and the breathing room after tasks when the plans feel too tight or too loose.

Tips and troubleshooting

Situation What to do
The badge says Offline or shows an error Check the connection, then Sync now on the card at the top of Settings. A token that expired or lost access shows an error there; make a new one.
Two devices show different things Wait a minute with the app open on both; every change syncs within a minute. Changes made at the same time are merged: the newest wins.
The app looks old New versions arrive on their own; a bar offers to reload when one arrives while the app is open. The bottom of Settings shows the version.
Someone else's data shows on a device Settings → Advanced → Empty this device, after checking that the repository there is the right one. If the repository itself was copied from someone else's, delete its files on GitHub first (for the calendar: Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar → Remove calendar data).
I'm looking for Import, or the full add menu Hold the + (right-click on a computer).
A task shows ⚠ Open it: it opens on the missing detail (a length, a place, the home address in Settings, or a travel time still being looked up).
Travel times are missing or wrong Settings → Travel: check the home address, the transport and Look up times again. Addresses need a street, number and city to be found.
The plan is always too full Settings → My day: lower Plan at most, narrow the suggestion hours, add breathing room after tasks.
An event is on the wrong day Open it from Events and change the date: its block and all its tied tasks move with it. On the Day view you can also drag the block.
I want my data Settings → Advanced → Download backup (JSON) gives one file with everything; your repository on GitHub holds the same files.
