Everything you need to set Everyday up and plan your days with it.

What Everyday is

Everyday is a planner for your days. There are two kinds of things in it: events, which already have a date (a concert, a visit, a class), and tasks, which still need one — you write them down and the app helps you decide when to do them. Every task takes some time, and once you put it on a day it becomes a block on that day's timeline, next to your events and calendar appointments. Tasks can also be tied to an event ("buy the tickets, two weeks before"), so the event carries its own to-do list. The app can suggest a plan for a day, a week or a month, keep Google Calendar in step, add the time it takes to get to a place, and plan meals and grocery runs together with the Ricette recipe app.

It works in the browser on a phone or a computer and can be installed like a normal app. Your data stays yours: it lives on your devices and in a private space on GitHub that only you can see. There is no account with us, no AI and nothing to pay.

What it is not: it is not a calendar (your appointments come from Google Calendar), not a notes app and not a tool for a team. One person, one set of data.

First setup

You need about fifteen minutes and a GitHub account (free). Everything happens in Settings: tap the gear at the top right. It has three cards — My day, Travel, Connections — and an Advanced fold for the rest.

1. Install the app

Open the app's address in Chrome on your phone, or in any browser on a computer. On Android choose Install app (or Add to Home screen) from the browser menu; on a computer click the small install icon in the address bar. The app works without a connection too; new versions arrive on their own, and a bar offers to reload. The bottom of Settings shows which version you have.

2. Connect your own space on GitHub

This is where your data is stored and how your devices stay in sync.

  1. On GitHub, create a private, empty repository (a "repository" is just a folder on GitHub), for example everyday-data. Don't copy anyone else's.
  2. Create a personal access token (GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens) that is allowed to read and write the Contents of that repository. A token is like a password made for apps.
  3. In Settings → Connections → GitHub sync, type your GitHub user name, the repository name and the token, then tap Save and test. The badge at the top of the app says Synced when it works.

The token stays on the device. Do step 3 on every phone or computer you use: they all share the same data, within a minute of any change.

3. Optional: Ricette (recipes and pantry)

If you also use the Ricette app, type the name of its data repository in Settings → Connections → Ricette (the same token must be allowed to use it). Everyday then knows your recipes and your pantry, shares the shopping list with Ricette and can plan meals from recipes.

4. Optional: Google Calendar

This link runs on GitHub, not on your phone, so it needs a one-time setup on Google's side. Settings → Connections → Google Calendar lists the steps; in short, you create a small Google Cloud project, allow it to use your calendar, and store three "secrets" in your repository. Once done, your appointments appear in the app and your plan appears in Google Calendar, with reminders.

5. Travel

In Settings → Travel, type your home address and how you usually get around (walking, bike, public transport, car). That's all the app needs to add travel time to tasks that have a place. A Google Maps key is optional; without one the app uses free map services.

6. A second person

Each person needs their own repository, their own token and (if they want it) their own calendar setup. On their device, open the same app address and type their repository in Settings → Connections. If the device held someone else's data before, the app asks whether to start empty — choose that. Empty this device, in the same section, does the same at any later time.

The four tabs

Tab What it is for
Today Home. The next move with its leave and start times, today's list, what is pressing, the week at a glance. Tapping the app's name brings you back here.
Events Everything with a fixed date, nearest first: your own events and the ones from Google Calendar, each with how ready it is.
Tasks Everything to do: To plan, Planned, Done, the Saved list of things that come back (gym, cleaning, meals), and the Shopping list. Search, filters, sorting, import from pasted text.
Planner Your plan as a Day, a Week or a Month, with a drawer of the tasks still to plan and the Plan button that lays them out for you.

The order is the order you plan in: see what's on today, put the dated things in, add the tasks around them, then let the Planner lay everything out. The gear at the top right opens Settings. On a phone, swipe left or right to move between the tabs. The badge next to the gear shows whether your data is in sync: Synced, Syncing, Offline, or Only on this device when you haven't connected yet.

Today

The next move card answers one question: when do I have to move? For a task with a place it shows two times — leave 11:00 → start 11:20 — and counts down to the leave time. On my way ticks the trip, Done finishes the task, the pin opens the address in Maps, Move opens the block. Below it, today's list shows every block and appointment in order (an orange time means "leave then"), and a line tells you how much free time you have until the next thing — tap it to see what fits.

Pressing lists what needs you: overdue tasks, tasks due in the next days that have no day yet, and tasks missing a detail. Today puts one in the next free slot; Fix opens it on the missing detail. The week strip at the bottom shows how full each day is; tap a day to open it in the Planner.

Every morning, the first time you open Today, what was left undone on earlier days goes back on the plan over the coming week; a line says how many things moved, with Undo.

Events

An event is something with a date you don't choose: a concert on the 20th of May, the dentist on Thursday at 9, Luca's wedding in June. You put it in as soon as you hear about it, and from then on it is on the plan, in Google Calendar, and on Today when the day comes. The planner never moves an event.

Adding an event

Type it in the field at the top of the Events tab: Concert 20 May 21:00 @Arena 3h, Dentist 12/11 at 9, Luca's wedding 12 Jun, Pottery class thu 18:30 2h. The app reads the date (20 May, 20/5, fri, tomorrow), the time (21:00, at 9), the place (@…) and the length (2h). Without a time it is an all-day event. Without a date the editor opens and asks for one — a date is the only thing an event needs.

Tap an event to open it. It is the same editor as a task, with the same parts in the same order; only the first one differs:

Part What you set there
When The date (required) and the time (leave it empty for all day); repetition for a weekly class; the reminder.
To do before and after The tasks tied to this event, with their lead time and whether each is planned — see below.
Length and category How long it takes (two hours until you say otherwise), a buffer after it, the category.
Place, Steps, Checklist, Notes Exactly as for a task: a place adds Get there with the real travel time, so Today shows leave 20:15 → start 21:00.

Tasks tied to an event

This is what makes events useful. In the event, + To do before / after adds a task with a lead time: Buy the tickets · 2 weeks before, Book the train · 1 week before, Thank Anna · 1 day after. Each one is a normal task: it appears in the Tasks tab (marked 📅 with the event's name), gets its due date from the event, shows up in Pressing when it slips, and the Planner places it like anything else. Move the event and all its tasks move with it.

Inside the event you see at a glance where you stand — 3 to do · 2 to plan, then ✓ all done — and Plan these lays the open ones out over the days before the event. The same badge shows in the Events list and on Today's next-move card. Open a tied task and its When part says For the event "Concert" · 2 weeks before; change the lead time there, or untie it.

Events imported from Google Calendar can take tied tasks too: open one from the Events list and add them the same way. The event itself stays Google's (you change it there), but its preparation lives here.

Events on the plan

On the Day timeline an event is a block with a 📅 and a stronger colour. Tapping an empty spot on the timeline makes a new event at that time (you picked a time, so it is fixed); + Add → Event does the same. You can still drag an event's block by hand; the planner, the morning re-flow and Plan leave events alone and work around them. Events go to Google Calendar like everything else, all-day ones as all-day entries.

Tasks

Adding a task

Type in the field at the top of the Tasks tab and press Enter. A few shortcuts save you from opening the task: Dentist 1h sets how long it takes, !1 to !5 how important it is (1 = very), #work the category, @Via Roma 12 the place, fri or tomorrow the day, by fri a due date, every mon a weekly repeat. With a day and a time (Call Marco tue 15:00) the task goes straight onto the plan. Import takes a whole list you paste in, one task per line.

Tap a task to open it. The task is split into parts you can fold; anything you don't touch keeps a sensible value.

Part What you set there
Length and importance How long it takes (30 minutes until you say otherwise), a buffer after it (a few spare minutes added to the block), how important it is, the category, and the Saved task switch.
When A day and a time, a due date, repetition, the part of the day you prefer, things that must come first, time windows (e.g. office hours), the reminder. For a task tied to an event: the event and the lead time instead of a due date.
Place An address or a link. With a place, the app adds Get there (and, if you like, Come back) with the real travel time.
Steps Parts of the task, each with its own length; on the plan every step gets its own time. Tap ⚓ on the step the task's time refers to (e.g. Workout at 19:00, so Get there goes before).
Checklist Things to tick while doing it.
Rules What should happen before or after, depending on how it goes (something to prepare before, a follow-up if it fails, a grocery run before a meal…). Shown when Rules, templates and preparations is on under Settings → Advanced.
Notes Free text.

Saved tasks

Things that come back without a fixed rhythm — gym, cleaning, meals — live under Saved. They are normal tasks; the only difference is that they stay in that list and you put them on a day when you want them, from + Add → Saved on the plan. Tick Saved task inside a task to move it there and back.

Recurring tasks

In the When part choose every day, weekdays, weekly (pick the days) or monthly, with every N and an end date if you like. Give the task a day and a time and every occurrence of the coming weeks goes on the plan by itself (marked ↻). Change one occurrence from its block, or the whole series from the task; when you change or delete from a block the app asks only this one or this and the following.

Finding things

The search field looks through titles, notes, places, steps and checklists. Filters: To fix shows the tasks the planner still needs a detail for (a length, a place, your home address…), Recurring, With a place, or one category. Compact shows one line per task when the list gets long. You can also sort: smart, your own order (drag ⠿), due date, importance, length, category, date added.

Planning

The views

The Planner shows your plan as a Day (a timeline from the start to the end of your day — you choose the hours in Settings → My day — with a red line for now), a Week (seven columns) or a Month (a grid). Every day has a small busy bar: how full it is compared with the daily limit you set. ‹ › move in time, Today brings you back, and tapping a day in the Week or Month opens it.

The drawer

At the bottom of the Planner sits To plan: the tasks that have no day yet. Open it to see them (search, sort, filter), tap ↓ on a row to put it in the next free slot of the day on screen, or hold and drag it onto the timeline, a week day or a month cell. The same drawer shows the previews when you let the app plan.

Putting a task on a day by hand

  • Tap an empty spot on the Day timeline: a new event at that time.
  • + Add → a Task (new or from the list), an Event, a Saved one or a Meal.
  • Drag a block to move it; drag its bottom edge to make it longer or shorter. Tap a block to tick it, see its steps and checklist, or change it.

Letting the app plan

✦ Plan is one guided flow. Pick the span (today, a few days, a week, two weeks, the month). If some tasks are missing a detail the planner needs, it asks right there — a length with one tap, a place, whether to split a task longer than a day's limit — no need to open them. Then the previews appear on the plan, inside your planning hours, respecting due dates, importance, your own order, time windows, things that must come first and the daily limit; each preview says why it landed where it did. Remove the ones you don't want, then Add in the drawer makes them real and the calendar follows. Tasks that are already planned are kept apart, so you don't plan them twice.

If a block overlaps a calendar event, or comes before something it depends on, a warning appears with a Fix button. A task with a ⚠ is missing a detail the planner wants: To fix in the filters lists them, and the Plan flow asks for them.

Travel

Give a task a place and the app adds Get there before it, with the travel time from your home (or from the place of the previous task that day, if you don't go home in between) and the transport you chose. Come back after it is optional. Both are steps of the task: you see them on the block, you can tick them, and the block grows around the task so the task itself stays at its time. Everywhere the block appears — Today, the Planner, Google Calendar — it reads with two times: leave 11:00 · 11:20–12:20.

In Settings → Travel → Journey legs you decide when each one is added: only when the task has a place, for every task (a task without a place then shows a "?" of 30 minutes as a reminder to account for the trip) or never (you switch it on per task). Each task can still say otherwise in its Place part, and pick its own transport.

The legs are saved tasks. Before the task (getting there) and After the task (coming back) each point to a saved task — Get there and Come back at first. You find them under Saved and can edit them from Settings: rename them, or give them steps — for example Pack the bag (5 min) and Travel. The step marked ⚓ (or the first one) receives the travel time from the map; the others keep their own length. You can also pick any other saved task for either leg.

Travel times come from free map services. With a Google Maps key in Settings, Google is used instead and public transport works everywhere. A "?" on a task means the time isn't known yet: the planner counts 30 minutes until it is, and ⚠ tells you when an address wasn't found. Start from where I am today uses your phone's location for the first trip of the day.

Meals, shopping list and grocery runs

With Ricette connected, + Add → Meal puts a meal on a day and lets you pick recipes for it; the preparation step takes its length from the recipes. When the time comes (or when you tick the check step) the app looks at the pantry: what's missing goes on the shopping list, and a grocery run is planned before the meal.

The Shopping part of the Tasks tab (To do · Saved · Shopping; the app remembers which one you used last, and a grocery-run block opens it directly) is the same list as in Ricette, both ways: add, change, tick or delete here and it shows there within a minute. Group it by department, recipe or shop, sort it A–Z or by when things were added, search it. Tap an item to change its quantity, note or the shops that have it.

Shops (Settings → Categories and shops) have opening hours and, if you like, an address. Say which shops carry an item and the grocery runs are planned when those shops are open, as one run or one per shop. A grocery run is a block like any other; ticking items as you buy them marks them bought in Ricette, and they go into the pantry when you put the shopping away there.

Google Calendar

Once set up, the link works both ways:

  • Your calendars → Everyday. Appointments from the calendars you import appear on the plan in their colour, as busy time the planner works around. You can't change them here; a link opens them in Google Calendar.
  • Everyday → Google Calendar. Everything you plan is written to a calendar called Everyday, with a reminder (the default from Settings → Connections → Google Calendar, or the task's own). A task with a trip is one event that starts when you leave, titled Dentist 11:20 · leave 11:00, so the reminder rings before you have to go. Your own events carry a 📅; all-day ones become all-day entries. Move or delete an Everyday event in Google Calendar and your plan follows.

The link refreshes every hour and a moment after you change your plan; Sync with Google now refreshes it by hand. The section in Settings shows when it last ran and how many appointments came in. Remove calendar data deletes the imported appointments from your repository — useful if a repository was copied from someone else's and shows their calendar.

A typical week

Sunday evening, ten minutes — left to right across the tabs. Open Events and put in anything with a date you heard about during the week (Dentist thu 9:00 @…, Dinner at Marco's sat 20:00); for the big ones add what has to happen before (+ To do before / after). Then Tasks: empty your head, with a length when you know it (Call the bank 20m, Tax return 2h !2), and a place for the things that happen somewhere. Put the week's meals on their days (+ Add → Meal) and pick the recipes. Then Planner → ✦ Plan → a week: answer the two or three details it asks, look at the previews, drag the one or two you want elsewhere, Add. Your appointments were already there, so nothing lands on top of them; the grocery run was placed before the first meal that needs it.

Every morning. Open the app: Today shows the next move with its leave time (an event's card also says how ready it is), the day's list, and anything that slipped from yesterday already back on the plan (Undo if you disagree). Google Calendar reminds you before each block. Pressing shows what needs a decision — one tap puts it on today. If the day looks too full, open the Planner and drag something to tomorrow.

During the day. Tick blocks as you finish them (for long ones, tick the steps). A meeting ended early? The free-time line on Today says how long you have and what fits. Something new came up? A date → Events (Concert 20 May 21:00); a thing to do → Tasks (call Marco tomorrow 15m), or put it on today from Pressing. Buy groceries from the Shopping tab, ticking items as they go into the basket.

Evening. What you didn't do stays open: tomorrow morning it is back on the plan, and the next Plan picks up the rest. Overdue tasks sit at the top of Pressing and of To plan, so they don't get lost.

Once in a while. Recurring things (gym Monday and Wednesday at 19:00, weekly cleaning) are set once and appear on their own; the Saved list holds what comes back without a rhythm. Settings → My day is where you adjust the day's hours, the daily limit and the breathing room after tasks when the plans feel too tight or too loose.

Tips and troubleshooting

Situation What to do
The badge says Offline or shows an error Check the connection, then Settings → Connections → Sync now. A token that expired or lost access shows an error there; make a new one.
Two devices show different things Wait a minute with the app open on both; every change syncs within a minute. Changes made at the same time are merged: the newest wins.
The app looks old New versions arrive on their own; a bar offers to reload when one arrives while the app is open. The bottom of Settings shows the version.
Someone else's data shows on a device Settings → Advanced → Empty this device, after checking that the repository there is the right one. If the repository itself was copied from someone else's, delete its files on GitHub first (for the calendar: Settings → Connections → Google Calendar → Remove calendar data).
A task shows ⚠ Open it: it opens on the missing detail (a length, a place, the home address in Settings, or a travel time still being looked up).
Travel times are missing or wrong Settings → Travel: check the home address, the transport and Look up times again. Addresses need a street, number and city to be found.
The plan is always too full Settings → My day: lower Plan at most, narrow the suggestion hours, add breathing room after tasks.
An event is on the wrong day Open it from Events and change the date: its block and all its tied tasks move with it. On the Day view you can also drag the block.
I want my data Settings → Advanced → Download backup gives one file with everything; your repository on GitHub holds the same files.
