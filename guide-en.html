What Everyday is

Everyday is a planner for your days. There are two kinds of things in it: events, which already have a date (a concert, a visit, a class), and tasks, which still need one — you write them down and the app helps you decide when to do them. Every task takes some time, and once you put it on a day it becomes a block on that day's timeline, next to your events and calendar appointments. Tasks can also be tied to an event ("buy the tickets, two weeks before"), so the event carries its own to-do list. The app can suggest a plan for a day, a week or a month, keep Google Calendar in step, add the time it takes to get to a place, and plan meals and grocery runs together with the Ricette recipe app.

It works in the browser on a phone or a computer and can be installed like a normal app. Your data stays yours: it lives on your devices and in a private space on GitHub that only you can see. There is no account with us, no AI and nothing to pay.

What it is not: it is not a calendar (your appointments come from Google Calendar), not a notes app and not a tool for a team. One person, one set of data.

First setup

You need about fifteen minutes and a GitHub account (free). Everything happens in Settings: tap the gear at the top right. Settings is a short list, and each item opens a page of its own: My day, Planner, Travel, Categories, Shops, Advanced (itself a list: the connections, the task list, rules and your data, each on a page of its own), Language and appearance (the language, and the theme: Automatic follows your phone or computer, or always Light or Dark) and the Guide. The card at the top says whether your data is in sync, with Sync now (or Connect, before you have).

1. Install the app

Open the app's address in Chrome on your phone, or in any browser on a computer. On Android choose Install app (or Add to Home screen) from the browser menu; on a computer click the small install icon in the address bar. The app works without a connection too; new versions arrive on their own, and a bar offers to reload. The bottom of Settings shows which version you have.

2. Connect your own space on GitHub

This is where your data is stored and how your devices stay in sync.

  1. On GitHub, create a private, empty repository (a "repository" is just a folder on GitHub), for example everyday-data. Don't copy anyone else's.
  2. Create a personal access token (GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens) that is allowed to read and write the Contents of that repository. A token is like a password made for apps.
  3. In Settings → Advanced → Sync with GitHub (or tap Connect on the card at the top of Settings), type your GitHub user name, the repository name and the token, then tap Save and test. The badge at the top of the app says Synced when it works.

The token stays on the device. Do step 3 on every phone or computer you use: they all share the same data, within a minute of any change.

3. Optional: Ricette (recipes and pantry)

If you also use the Ricette app, type the name of its data repository in Settings → Advanced → Ricette (the same token must be allowed to use it). Everyday then knows your recipes and your pantry, shares the shopping list with Ricette and can plan meals from recipes.

4. Optional: Palestra (workouts)

If you use the Palestra workout app, type the name of its data repository in Settings → Advanced → Palestra (usually palestra-data; the same token must be allowed to use it). Its workouts then show up as routines, planned on the days of Palestra's active workout (its plan for the week), and every workout you finish there comes back with its real time. See Workouts from Palestra.

5. Optional: Google Calendar

This link runs on GitHub, not on your phone, so it needs a one-time setup on Google's side. Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar lists the steps; in short, you create a small Google Cloud project, allow it to use your calendar, and store three "secrets" in your repository. Once done, your appointments appear in the app and your plan appears in Google Calendar, with reminders.

6. Travel

In Settings → Travel, type your home address and how you usually get around (walking, bike, public transport, car). That's all the app needs to add travel time to tasks that have a place. A Google Maps key is optional; without one the app uses free map services.

7. A second person

Each person needs their own repository, their own token and (if they want it) their own calendar setup. On their device, open the same app address and type their repository in Settings → Advanced → Connections. If the device held someone else's data before, the app asks whether to start empty — choose that. Empty this device, at the bottom of the same page, does the same at any later time.

The three tabs

On a computer the three tabs sit in the rail on the left, with To do's views and this week's days under them, and the pane on the right shows what you opened. The + sits at the bottom right of the tab.

The round + at the bottom right adds something from every tab; see Adding things. The order of the tabs is the order you plan in: see what's on today, put the dated things in, add the tasks around them, then let the Planner lay everything out. The gear at the top right opens Settings. On a phone, swipe left or right to move between the tabs. The badge next to the gear shows whether your data is in sync: Synced, Syncing, Offline, or Only on this device when you haven't connected yet.

Today

The next move card answers one question: when do I have to move? For a task with a place it shows two times — leave 11:00 → start 11:20 — and counts down to the leave time. On my way ticks the trip, Done finishes the task, the pin opens the address in Maps, Move opens the block. Below it, today's list shows every block and appointment in order (an orange time means "leave then"), and a line tells you how much free time you have until the next thing — tap it to see what fits.

Pressing lists what needs you: overdue tasks, tasks due in the next days that have no day yet, and tasks missing a detail. Today puts one in the next free slot; Fix opens it on the missing detail. The week strip at the bottom shows how full each day is; tap a day to open it in the Planner.

To plan this week. For a repeating task whose time you pick each week (see Recurring tasks), a card at the top of Today offers free slots for each day it's due: Run · Tomorrow with 17:30, 19:15, 09:00 or Other…. Tap a time to put it there, Skip to leave that day out, or Fix the highlighted ones to take the first offer for all of them. The card for the week ahead appears on Sunday at 18:00; you can move that to another day or time in Settings → My day → Card "To plan this week". Today's occurrences always show. On the weekly card, Review the week opens Routine on the week ahead, so you can see everything at once: what's in place, what's still to plan, what to skip or pause.

Every morning, the first time you open Today, what was left undone on earlier days goes back on the plan over the coming week; a line says how many things moved, with Undo.

A task that takes more than one day. If a day ends with only some of a task's steps ticked — say 3 of 8 — that day isn't moved: it stays on the plan as a trace, striped, with ◐ 3/8 and the steps you ticked. Only the 5 steps left are planned again, as a new block marked day 2 (then day 3, and so on). So looking back, you see every day the task was on your plate and what you did on each. A day where you didn't get to it at all simply moves, as before. Open the task and it says ◐ 3 of 8 steps done · on your plan since Mon 26 Oct · 3 days, with the done steps marked ✓.

Doing a task: the timer

▶ Start now is in every task and in every block of today. The block moves to now (or a new one starts now), and a bar at the top of the app shows the task and the time running: Water the plants · since 08:30 · planned 10m · 0:07. Pause stops the clock while you're interrupted; Done ends it. The block then keeps the real time it took, and the task remembers how long it usually takes you (Real time · usually 25m in the task), which the planner uses next time. One timer runs at a time; starting another ends the first.

If you start late and the task now runs into what comes next, Today says so and offers Move the next ones to the planner's next good slots (with Undo). If you finish early, it offers Move up for the next task of the day, into the time you freed.

Appointments and events

An appointment is something with a day and a time you don't choose: the dentist on Thursday at 9, a concert on the 20th of May, Luca's wedding in June. There is no separate tab any more: an appointment is in the Planner, on its day's agenda, together with your Google Calendar events (all-day ones on top). The planner never moves it and builds around it.

Adding an appointment

With the +, like everything else: type the name and turn on 📅 It’s an appointment. When opens on I choose · Once already: pick the day and the Start (the End follows the length). Without a time it is all day. Holding the + also offers Appointment, which opens the same sheet with the toggle on.

Tap an appointment in the Planner to open it. It is the same editor as a task, with the same parts in the same order; only the first one differs:

Part What you set
When The date (required) and the time (empty = all day); repeating, for a weekly class; the reminder.
Before and after The tasks tied to it, with how far ahead and whether each is planned — see below.
Length and category How long it lasts (an hour until you say otherwise), a buffer after, the category.
Place, Steps, Checklist, Notes Exactly as for a task: a place adds Getting there with the real travel time, so Today shows leave 20:15 → start 21:00.

Tasks tied to an event

This is what makes events useful. In the event, + To do before / after adds a task with a lead time: Buy the tickets · 2 weeks before, Book the train · 1 week before, Thank Anna · 1 day after. Each one is a normal task: it appears in To do (marked 📅 with the event's name), gets its due date from the event, shows up in Pressing when it slips, and the Planner places it like anything else. Move the event and all its tasks move with it.

Inside the event you see at a glance where you stand — 3 to do · 2 to plan, then ✓ all done — and Plan these lays the open ones out over the days before the event. The same badge shows and on Today's next-move card. Open a tied task and its When part says For the event "Concert" · 2 weeks before; change the lead time there, or untie it.

Events imported from Google Calendar can take tied tasks too: tap it in the Planner and add them the same way. The event itself stays Google's (you change it there), but its preparation lives here.

Events on the plan

On the Day agenda an event is a row with a 📅 and a stronger colour (in the By the hour layout, a block you can drag by hand; there, tapping an empty spot on the timeline makes an event at that time); the planner, the morning re-flow and Plan leave events alone and work around them. Events go to Google Calendar like everything else, all-day ones as all-day entries. An event with a time is always one block: change it after the fact and that block changes; delete the event and it leaves the plan, past days included.

To do

Views

To do holds everything you have to do: the one-off things, the ones that come back (routines) and the shopping. It isn't split into fixed lists: you look at it through views. A view is a set of filters with a name; the ones you tick are the tabs on top, three to a row, wrapping instead of scrolling. The starting views are Overview, This week (today and this week), Shopping, Workouts (Health) and Home; To plan, Meals, Work and Personal are there but not ticked.

Under the tabs, one line says what the view shows: Shows: Home. Then the toolbar, the same in every view: search, Filters and ▤ Sort and group.

  • Filters opens a sheet with Kind (once, repeats), State (to plan, planned, done), When (today, this week, later, no date) and Group (your categories, and No group). Closed, the button only says how many are on: Filters · 2. If you changed a view's filters, As before goes back to the saved ones and Keep for this view saves them. Type a name below and tap Save as view to make a new view of them.
  • ▤ Sort and group picks Date added, When, Group or Kind. Tap the chosen one again to reverse it: the button then says Newest first or Oldest first, or shows ↑ ↓. By group, the order is the one in Settings → Categories (drag them to change it), with No group first.
  • ✎ Views, the last box of the tabs, ticks views on and off the tabs, moves them with ▲ ▼, and with ⋯ renames or deletes them. Deleting a view deletes nothing that was in it. Overview always stays first.

Overview and Review

Overview is the list of everything, one row each, newest first. It's where you throw things in and sort them later. Tap a row to sort it there: for what misses a group, a when (for one-off things) or a length, + Category, + When and + Length appear, and Details opens the task.

When there are some, a card on top says 4 to plan with Review: it takes them one at a time, with the categories (tap one again to take it off), the lengths and the when (Today, Tomorrow, This week, Next week, No date, Choose…) one tap away; what you already chose is marked. Next goes on, Delete task removes it, Close stops for now.

Adding things

Tap the round +. One sheet opens, for everything: on top the name (What do you need to do?) with ☆ Save next to it, to keep it among your saved tasks and do it again whenever. Below, the 📅 It's an appointment toggle: a time of yours that doesn't move (the dentist, a dinner). Then three pills, which open below without leaving the sheet:

  • ⏱ Length — a number and a unit (minutes, hours, days, weeks, months). You can also type 2h30: it becomes 2.5 hours. Don't know is fine: when you mark it done, the outcome asks how long did it take? and next time the app knows. Something that lasts several days is split into parts.
  • 🕒 When — ✦ First free time (the first free slot; the button says where), 📅 I choose or To decide (stays to plan). With I choose: Once (a day, or no date) or Several times (3 a day, 2 a week, 1 year: the field understands and the menu next to it follows; then the days and for how long). The time has Start and End linked to the length: set two and the third follows; tap a box's name to keep it fixed (the highlighted one). Several times a day: a Start → End row for each.
  • ● Category — tap a colour.

Opening a pill, seeing the choice already there and closing it again is enough: that choice sticks.

More options opens below, in the same style: importance, deadline, place, steps, Needs first… and the rules (If done…, If it fails…) — for these two type to search, tap a suggestion or ✚ New — and notes.

The button at the bottom says what it will do: Add (to plan), Put in the first free slot · Wed 09:00, Put on the plan · Tomorrow · 10:00–11:00. Enter in the name does the same. Shortcuts typed in the name still work: Dentist 1h, #work, by fri, every thu, 3 times a week. Anything that repeats becomes a routine by itself. A new task shows at the top of its list under Just added, and for a few seconds a bar says where it went, with Undo.

Meal opens the meal editor.

Hold the + (or right-click on a computer) for the full menu: Task, Event, From a routine, Meal and Import a list, which takes a whole list you paste, one task per line.

In views tied to another app, the + also has on top what you do there. In Workouts: ▶ Plan a workout (pick one of Palestra's workouts, then the day and the slot) and New in Palestra ↗. In Meals: ▶ Plan a meal and New in Ricette ↗. What you type in the field below stays only in Everyday. In Shopping the + adds to the list (shared with Ricette when connected).

Tap a task to open it. The task is split into parts you can fold; anything you don't touch keeps a sensible value.

Part What you set there
Length and importance How long it takes (30 minutes until you say otherwise), a buffer after it (a few spare minutes added to the block), how important it is, one or more categories (the first one gives the colour). For a routine on several weekdays, Different length on some days (see Recurring tasks).
When First one choice: Once, Repeats or Whenever (see below). That choice says whether it's a task or a routine: Repeats and Whenever are routines; then only what that needs: a day and a time, a due date, the part of the day you prefer, things that must come first (type a few letters to find a task, or make a new one on the spot), time windows (e.g. office hours), the reminder. For a task tied to an event: the event and the lead time instead of a due date.
Place An address or a link. With a place, the app adds Get there (and, if you like, Come back) with the real travel time.
Steps Parts of the task, each with its own length; on the plan every step gets its own time. Tap ⚓ on the step the task's time refers to (e.g. Workout at 19:00, so Get there goes before).
Checklist Things to tick while doing it.
Rules What should happen before or after, depending on how it goes (something to prepare before, a follow-up if it fails, a grocery run before a meal…). Shown when Rules, templates and preparations is on under Settings → Advanced; day templates and preparations are then listed on that page too.
Notes Free text.

Saved and routines

Routines are what comes back: cleaning, the washing, picking the children up from school, the gym, the run, meals. Some have a fixed rhythm, some come back now and then. You don't need them every day; you look at them once a week and adjust. Everything set to Repeats or Whenever in When is a routine; Once is a task. They show in the views with everything else (filter Kind · Repeats); to have them all together, pick that filter and save it as a view.

Saved. The Saved view (it was Routine) gathers what you keep to do again. On top ↻ Repeating: the routines, with their week. Below To do again whenever: saved things with no rhythm, with how long they take, last time 12 Oct and ▶ to plan them. You no longer choose «routine» or «once»: anything that repeats is a routine by itself. A one-off thing goes into Saved only if you tap ☆ Save in the +; once done, it stays there for next time.

If some saved routine has no rhythm (so it never shows among the things to plan), the view says so with a card: for each one 1× a week or ✕, or All once a week or They’re fine like this. Those that had days but had lost their rhythm go back to weekly by themselves.

Where there are routines, the view also reads by the week. On top: Week 2–8 Nov, with how it's going (9 of 12 in place · 2 to plan); ‹ › go to other weeks, and tapping the week comes back to this one. Each routine has its seven days, with these marks:

Mark Means
● full done
○ ring on the plan
dashed square to plan: it's due that day, the time isn't picked yet
– dash skipped (or paused)
crossed ring missed: the day passed and it didn't happen

A routine without a rhythm has a ▶ instead, to plan it (pick a day and a slot); holding the + and From a routine does the same. The + with Repeats adds a new routine (Bins out every thu 10m).

Workouts from Palestra

With Palestra connected, its workouts show in the Workouts view (the Health group); the + there has ▶ Plan a workout and New in Palestra ↗. Palestra decides their name, length and exercises; Everyday decides when. Palestra's active workout, its plan for the week, sets the rhythm:

  • A workout on fixed weekdays repeats on those days, at the workout's time if it has one, otherwise you pick the time each week on the Sunday To plan card.
  • A workout on "any day" twice in the plan becomes 2 times a week, also picked on the card.
  • Only the active workout's routines (and those of one starting later) show in Everyday. The others stay out of the lists, with their past sessions, and come back when you put them in a workout.
  • A workout made active "from Monday" takes over on Monday; until then the old one keeps going. When the workout changes, what the old one had put on the plan from that day on comes off; sessions already done stay.
  • Moving a session to tomorrow in Palestra moves the block in Everyday too, at the next sync: at the same time if it's free, otherwise in the first free slot that day. Moving it again follows step by step, and undoing the move in Palestra brings it back. A session already started or done doesn't move.
  • A workout deleted in Palestra moves to Archived: its past days stay, and it's not planned any more.

On the day, the workout's card on Today, its block and its editor say ▶ Start in Palestra: that opens Palestra on that workout and starts Everyday's timer at the same moment (the bar at the top gets a Palestra ↗ link back). While the workout is going on in Palestra (even if you started it there), Everyday sees it within a minute: that day's block starts at the real time, with the bar on top in Palestra since 18:35 and ● in progress in Palestra; a pause in Palestra is a pause here too. If there was no block, a temporary one shows up. When you finish in Palestra, the session comes back at the next sync: the block takes the real start and end, and the minutes you trained without the pauses. A workout you did in Palestra without starting it here lands on the plan by itself, on that day's block for the same workout or as a new one. Everyday in turn tells Palestra what's planned for the next two weeks, so Palestra shows your next workout.

Tap a routine to see how it's been going and change what you change from time to time:

  • Recent weeks: the last three weeks and this one, with 8 of 10 done.
  • When: the rhythm, the time, the length (it opens the When part, see below).
  • Period: from when and until when, e.g. the school run from 14 Sep to 30 Jun. Nothing is planned outside it.
  • Pauses: a stretch of time when it rests (the Christmas holidays, two weeks of illness). Pick the start and 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks or an end date; leave the end empty to pause until you take it off. Whatever was planned in a pause comes off the plan, and it comes back by itself after.
  • Skip a day this week: one tap takes that day off the plan.
  • More details opens the full editor.

Recurring tasks

At the top of the card is all you need for the when, without opening anything. Under Once · Repeats · Whenever: for Once the day (Today, Tomorrow or a date) and the time; for Repeats the seven days to tap (none is on by itself: until you pick one the line says Pick the days), with Every day, Weekdays, Weekend, N times a week and Every month, and the time — typing a time means Always at this time, or I pick it each week; then the length. One line sums it up: ↻ Every day · 20:30 · 1h 30m · goes on the plan by itself, and names the days that differ, e.g. Thu: 20:00 · 1h. Dinner every day at 20:30 is: Repeats, Every day, 20:30. Different times on different days… opens the week grid. Meal types (Breakfast, Dinner…) can repeat this way too, and each time is a meal on the plan; their time (Breakfast 07:30) is already there when you switch to Repeats. Under On the plan, ✕ takes off just that time (for a routine with fixed times, that day is skipped), with Undo. How often, the end, pauses and the rest are further down, under More → Repeat and due date.

The When part starts with one choice:

  • Once: a day and a time, or neither (the planner finds a slot), and a due date if there is one.
  • Repeats: Every day, Some days (pick the days, e.g. Tue and Thu), N times a week or Every month, with Every N and an end date if you like. Then And the time?:
  • Always at the same time: every occurrence of the coming weeks goes on the plan by itself (marked ↻).
  • I pick it each week: the days are fixed, the time isn't. The To plan this week card on Today offers free slots for each day (see Today). N times a week always works this way.
  • Whenever: no day; it waits on the Planner's Routines shelf until you plan it (handy for routines without a rhythm).

For how long? A repeating routine can end: Always, For … weeks, For … times or Until…. With For 10 times the turns you do count: when you mark Done, the block's Outcome says Counts toward the 10 times with a switch, so a turn done halfway can count, or a done one can not. An ended routine leaves the plan and the lists; you find it under Ended, where you can pick it up again.

Change one occurrence from its block, or the whole series from the task; when you change or delete from a block the app asks only this one or this and the following.

Different lengths and times by day. Cleaning takes 45 minutes on Tuesday and Thursday, 2 hours on Saturday (and maybe starts at 10 on Saturdays). There are two ways to say so:

  • From the block. Change a Saturday's length (or its time, or both) and Save. The app asks what it's for: Every Saturday (the main choice: the other days stay as they are), Only this one (Sat 7 Nov), or All of them, from here on. Undo on the bar takes it back. For a routine whose time you pick each week, a new time is already for that turn only and doesn't ask.
  • In the routine. Tap the length (⏱) and turn on Different length on some days: one length appears for each of the routine's days. The length button then shows the summary, Tue, Thu: 45m · Sat: 2h, and so does the routine's card.

Smart learning. Turn it on (Settings → My day) and the app looks at the timer's real times: when a routine took much more or much less than planned on a weekday 3 times in a row, the block's Outcome says so — Thursdays take you longer · The last 3: 1h 12m, 1h 15m, 1h 10m — and offers Use 1h 15m on Thursdays. Leave it won't ask again on that block; Don't ask again turns it off for that routine. Without the timer nothing happens; it's off until you turn it on.

Finding things

Search, on top of every view, looks in titles, notes, places, categories, steps and checklists, within what the view shows. Filters narrows it down, ▤ sorts and groups (see Views). Shopping has its own ways to see the list, see Meals, shopping list and grocery runs.

Every row reads the same way: the title, one line of details (day, length, place, category) and the importance as orange exclamation marks on the right. A ⚠ means the planner misses a detail.

Planning

The views

The Planner shows your plan as a Day, a Week or a Month. The Day is an agenda: things in time order, each with its start and end on the left and its length as a number and a thin bar. Between them, a dashed row says how much free time there is: Free · 2h 20m · what fits? (within the hours the planner may use, Settings → Planner). The Week is a list of the seven days, each with its load bar and the day's things as small labels with the time; tap a day to open it. The Month is a grid.

A long block with other things inside (2 hours or more) becomes a box that holds them: what falls in its hours is listed inside the box, which ends with 21:00 · end. If the block lets other things happen (an event shown as free in Google, or a block that may overlap), its free time shows inside too. ▴ close folds it to one line saying what's inside.

If you prefer the hour timeline (blocks to drag, and the To plan drawer at the bottom), choose Settings → Planner → Planner layout → By the hour.

The free time

Tap what fits? on a free row: the things still to plan that fit open as a list, and one tap puts one there, at that time.

Plan: two ways

One card on top of the Day and the Week says what's left: 9 to plan (tasks without a day and this week's routine turns still without a time). On a phone Plan asks how:

  • ✦ Smart suggestion: the app finds free time for all of them; you look at the proposals and add them (see Letting the app plan).
  • I choose: search, pick something, then where.

I choose: placing things by hand

I choose opens a list with search on top and four tabs: To plan, Routines (this week's turns, with how many times each is already on the plan, and the whenever routines), On the plan (to find and move what's already there) and All. Tap something: a bar on top says Tap a lit free stretch for “Call Marco” (20m), and the free stretches it fits in light up on the agenda. Tap one and it goes there. A routine turn keeps its day; put it on another day and the app asks whether it goes Instead of a turn that week or as One more. From a routine's card, + One more time… does the same: pick where, and it goes on once more. You can put it in a stretch that's too short too (it says put it here (too short); on a computer by dragging as well): it goes there, and the bar at the bottom warns — ⚠ “Long job” and “Evening” overlap, or how much time is missing. ✕ on the bar stops.

On a time that's taken. While you place something, blocks not done yet and events are dashed. Tap one (or, on a computer, drop what you're dragging on it): a sheet puts the two side by side and asks — Right after it (the main choice: the other stays where it is), Instead of … (it takes that time, then you say where the other goes: To the first free time, I choose where, or Back to To plan; for a routine turn To plan again or Skip this turn), or Together, in parallel (both at that time, no warning for these two). One Undo puts it all back: with I choose where too, the bar comes at the end (2 moved · …) and Undo all puts both back. For a routine turn, first free time stays in its part of the day (a dinner, the evening). Your own Google event doesn't move from here, so there it's right after and together; on an invitation there's also Not going · put it here, see Google Calendar.

Other ways:

  • Tap what fits? in a free stretch: it lists the tasks and routines that fit (see above).
  • The + adds a task (see Adding things): ✦ First free time puts it in the first free slot, 📅 I choose lets you pick. Hold the + for an event, a routine or a meal.
  • On a computer, drag something from the pane on the right onto a free stretch, another day, or a block to take its place. A block on the agenda can be dragged to another stretch too, or back onto the pane: it leaves the plan and is to plan again (with Undo). Appointments and blocks with an Outcome stay put. When nothing is left to plan, the pane's To plan tab goes away.

Left behind. A block of today whose time is over with no Outcome says ⏰ past, and a card at the top of the day gathers them all — ⏰ 2 things left behind today — with ✓ (done), ✕ (didn't manage) and → later (to the first free time from now), each with Undo. The yellow number on the Planner icon counts them even when you're on another tab.

The numbers on the Planner icon. Red: conflicts from now to the end of the week. Yellow: things left behind. Green: what's still to plan. In Settings → Planner choose which to show (or none) and whether times are 24-hour (the default, 14:30) or 12-hour (2:30 pm).

A block opened, and its Outcome

Tap something on the agenda to open it. The time reads Start → End, with the length below: one of the two is fixed (it has the lit ring; when the sheet opens it's the Start). Tap Start or End to choose which; a length then counts from there — with the End fixed at 14:00, 1h 30m makes it start at 12:30. Tap a time to type it: typing the Start moves the block (the end follows), typing the End changes the length. Below is the rest of the day: your other blocks in grey, events in their colour, this block in teal. If it overlaps something you see it striped, with Overlaps … and Move to … (the first free time it fits in); you can also drag the block along the strip. Hide takes the strip away (Show the day brings it back), and the app remembers.

Then steps and checklist, notes. At the bottom is the Outcome: ▶ Start (today only: the timer starts), ✓ Done and ✕ Didn't manage. Afterwards the Outcome says how it went (Done · took 1h 12m, planned 40m) with Undo.

Letting the app plan

✦ Smart suggestion is one guided flow, with the same name on a phone and a computer. Pick the span (the day you're looking at — Only Thu 5 —, a few days, a week, two, the month). Routine turns and tasks are planned together, so they don't overlap, and only on the days you chose; the count is the same everywhere, and every proposal has ✓ and ✕, in List too. If some tasks miss a detail the planner needs, it asks right there — a length in one tap, a place, whether to split a task longer than the daily limit — without opening them. Then the proposals appear dashed in their place on the agenda, the times proposed for routine turns without a time too, and the card becomes 7 proposals · Cancel · Add. Each proposal has ✓ (puts it on the plan at once) and ✕ (takes it back); Add puts all the ones left on the plan (after a ✓ it says Add the rest), Cancel drops the ones left and keeps those already taken.

If a block overlaps a calendar event, or comes before something it depends on, a warning appears: tap it for the list. Fix lets the app choose; for an overlap, Choose puts the two side by side with Keep this, then asks where the other goes (first free time, I choose where, Back to To plan); a Google event always stays put. It's fine (or Leave them overlapping) drops the warning for those two only: if the same block also overlaps something else, that warning stays; and if you move the block later, the warning comes back.

A task with a ⚠ is missing a detail the planner wants: To fix in the filters lists them, and the Plan flow asks for them.

Travel

Give a task a place and the app adds Get there before it, with the travel time from your home (or from the place of the previous task that day, if you don't go home in between) and the transport you chose. Come back after it is optional. Both are steps of the task: you see them on the block, you can tick them, and the block grows around the task so the task itself stays at its time. Everywhere the block appears — Today, the Planner, Google Calendar — it reads with two times: leave 11:00 · 11:20–12:20.

In Settings → Travel → Journey legs you decide when each one is added: only when the task has a place, for every task (a task without a place then shows a "?" of 30 minutes as a reminder to account for the trip) or never (you switch it on per task). Each task can still say otherwise in its Place part, and pick its own transport.

The legs are tasks of their own. Before the task (getting there) and After the task (coming back) each point to a task — Get there and Come back at first. You edit them from Settings → Travel: rename them, or give them steps — for example Pack the bag (5 min) and Travel. The step marked ⚓ (or the first one) receives the travel time from the map; the others keep their own length. You can also pick any routine for either leg.

Travel times come from free map services. With a Google Maps key in Settings, Google is used instead and public transport works everywhere. A "?" on a task means the time isn't known yet: the planner counts 30 minutes until it is, and ⚠ tells you when an address wasn't found. Start from where I am today uses your phone's location for the first trip of the day.

Meals, shopping list and grocery runs

With Ricette connected, + Add → Meal puts a meal on a day and lets you pick recipes for it; the preparation step takes its length from the recipes. When the time comes (or when you tick the check step) the app looks at the pantry: what's missing goes on the shopping list, and a grocery run is planned before the meal.

When something runs out. If a meal ticked done finishes something in the pantry (the milk at 0, the eggs under their threshold), the app asks as Ricette does: milk: finished · 🛒 Add 500 ml / No, thanks, with − / + on the amount; for several things, Add them all. No, thanks doesn't ask again until the product is back in the pantry. From now on, add them by itself changes the choice. There is one choice for both apps: in Ricette (Settings → Pantry and reminders) or here in Settings → Ricette → When something runs out: Ask me, Add it by itself (onto the list at once, with Undo) or Do nothing.

With Ricette connected, you keep the list in Ricette. The Shopping view of To do only shows it, as Palestra does for workouts: To buy · 5 · from Ricette, by department, with no + and no ticks. ✎ Edit in Ricette ↗ opens the list there. Below are Trips in the plan (tap one to open it) and ▶ Plan a trip.

How did it go? A grocery run on the Planner is a block like the others: open it to see what to buy there, then the Outcome.

  • ✓ Got everything: done, and everything on the trip counts as bought in Ricette too (with Undo). When you put the shopping away in Ricette, the list empties by itself.
  • Something was missing: tick what you got (it goes to Ricette too: the one thing Everyday writes on the list), then ↻ Another trip for 3 things or That’s fine.
  • ✕ I didn’t go: the list stays to buy, and ↻ Another trip is right there.

Another trip offers ✦ First free time (with the shops open), With the next shopping if one is already planned, or 📅 I choose when… (a new trip whose day and time you set).

Without Ricette the list is Everyday's own, and you keep it here:

  • Adding. Tap + and type 2 l milk, dish soap or shampoo; Enter adds each one and keeps the sheet open, so you can type the whole list in one go. Each item lands in its department by itself — and in the same one whatever language you type in (cavolfiore, Blumenkohl and cauliflower all go under Fruit & veg). Household and personal care have departments of their own. Items Ricette adds because something is running low carry a running low label.
  • ⇅ View. The button next to the search shows the current view; tap it to choose Departments, Recipes, Shops, My order, A–Z, Newest first or Oldest first, and to filter: only what you can get at one shop, or only what is running low. Active filters show as chips under the search; tap one to remove it.
  • Ticking. Tick an item at the shop and it leaves its group for the cart at the bottom: In the cart · 3. Tap the count to see what's in it; Put in pantry puts everything into Ricette's pantry: food as always, household, care and baby things in the place Ricette gives their department (usually Household and Baby).
  • An item. Tap its name to change the name, the quantity (− / +), the unit, the department, the note and the shops that have it; + New shop adds one right there. Delete is in the same sheet, with Undo.
  • ⋯ More. Plan the shopping (the grocery runs), Share the list, Select (tick several items to give them shops or delete them together), Reorder (drag ⠿ to put departments and items in the order of your supermarket; My order always has the handles) and Empty the cart.

Shops (Settings → Shops) have opening hours and, if you like, an address. Say which shops carry an item and the grocery runs are planned when those shops are open, as one run or one per shop. A grocery run is a block like any other; it lists what to buy there, and ticking items as you buy them marks them bought in Ricette too.

Google Calendar

Once set up, the link works both ways:

  • Your calendars → Everyday. Appointments from the calendars you import appear on the plan in their colour, as busy time the planner works around (those set to Free in Google say free in Google, and their time stays free). You can't change them here; a link opens them in Google Calendar.
  • Everyday → Google Calendar. Everything you plan is written to a calendar called Everyday, with a reminder (the default from Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar, or the task's own). A task with a trip is one event that starts when you leave, titled Dentist 11:20 · leave 11:00, so the reminder rings before you have to go. Your own events carry a 📅; all-day ones become all-day entries. Move or delete an Everyday event in Google Calendar and your plan follows.

Invitations. An event someone else invited you to shows what you answered: ✉ Invite · not answered, ✉ Maybe or ✓ You're going. Events you made yourself (or with no guests) have no tag. If you won't go to one:

  • By putting something in its place. While you place something (see I choose), invitations are dashed too: tap one and choose Not going · put … here. If you had said yes, an amber note reminds you. Answer No on Google too (off until you tick it) sends your No to whoever invited you at the next sync. One Undo puts everything back.
  • Without placing anything. Open the invitation: it says who invited you and your answer, and has Not going (with the same box for Google).

An invitation you're not going to stays in the day, grey and struck through with Not going, and no longer counts as busy: the planner can use the time, and on the agenda it's part of the free stretch. Open it for I'm going after all (whatever took its place moves to the first free time) or Remove from the day (gone from here, still in Google Calendar). If you answered No on Google, it stays No there: change it in Google Calendar.

The link refreshes every hour and a moment after you change your plan; Sync with Google now refreshes it by hand. The section in Settings shows when it last ran and how many appointments came in. Remove calendar data deletes the imported appointments from your repository — useful if a repository was copied from someone else's and shows their calendar.

A typical week

Sunday evening, ten minutes — left to right across the tabs. With the + (📅 It’s an appointment) put in anything with a date you heard about during the week (Dentist thu 9:00 @…, Dinner at Marco's sat 20:00); for the big ones add what has to happen before (+ To do before / after). Then the +: empty your head, with a length when you know it (Call the bank 20m, Tax return 2h !2), and a place for the things that happen somewhere. Put the week's meals on their days (+ Add → Meal) and pick the recipes. If the To plan this week card is up, tap the times for the gym and the runs. Then Planner → Plan → ✦ Smart suggestion → a week: answer the two or three details it asks, look at the previews, drag the one or two you want elsewhere, Add. Your appointments were already there, so nothing lands on top of them; the grocery run was placed before the first meal that needs it.

Every morning. Open the app: Today shows the next move with its leave time (an event's card also says how ready it is), the day's list, and anything that slipped from yesterday already back on the plan (Undo if you disagree). Google Calendar reminds you before each block. Pressing shows what needs a decision — one tap puts it on today. If the day looks too full, open the Planner and move something to tomorrow. An invitation you no longer want? Put what you have to do on top of it, with Not going.

During the day. Tap ▶ Start now when you begin something, and Done on the bar when you finish; or just tick blocks as you finish them (for long ones, tick the steps). A meeting ended early? The free-time line on Today says how long you have and what fits. Something new came up? Tap +: call Marco tomorrow 15m, or tap Event for Concert 20 May 21:00. Buy groceries from the Shopping tab, ticking items as they go into the basket.

Evening. What you didn't do stays open: tomorrow morning it is back on the plan, and the next Plan picks up the rest. Overdue tasks sit at the top of Pressing and of To plan, so they don't get lost.

Once in a while. Recurring things (gym Monday and Wednesday at 19:00, weekly cleaning) are set once and appear on their own; for the ones whose time changes, I pick it each week brings them to the Sunday card. Routine shows them all by the week, with pauses for the holidays. Settings → My day is where you adjust the day's hours, the daily limit and the breathing room after tasks when the plans feel too tight or too loose.

On a computer

On a wide screen (1100 pixels and more) the app uses the sides. On the left is a rail with the four tabs, under To do its views with how many things each holds (and the number to plan on Overview), and this week's days with how full they are: tap a day to open it in the Planner. The tabs at the bottom and the view tabs on top aren't needed any more and go away.

On the right is a pane. On To do, tapping a row opens it there instead of in a sheet over the list: group, when and length in one tap, the main details, Plan…, Done and All details for the full editor. On the Planner it always shows I choose (search, and the tabs To plan, Routines, On the plan, All): pick something and the free time it fits in lights up on the agenda (put it here); tap it and it goes there, or drag the thing where you want it. Plan… in To do's pane leads to the same place. On Today the pane says what's waiting: what's to plan and the coming events.

On a phone, or with a narrow window, everything stays as it is.

Tips and troubleshooting

Situation What to do
The badge says Offline or shows an error Check the connection, then Sync now on the card at the top of Settings. A token that expired or lost access shows an error there; make a new one.
Two devices show different things Wait a minute with the app open on both; every change syncs within a minute. Changes made at the same time are merged: the newest wins.
The app looks old New versions arrive on their own; a bar offers to reload when one arrives while the app is open. The bottom of Settings shows the version.
Someone else's data shows on a device Settings → Advanced → Your data → Empty this device, after checking that the repository there is the right one. If the repository itself was copied from someone else's, delete its files on GitHub first (for the calendar: Settings → Advanced → Google Calendar → Remove calendar data).
I'm looking for Import, or the full add menu Hold the + (right-click on a computer).
A task shows ⚠ Open it: it opens on the missing detail (a length, a place, the home address in Settings, or a travel time still being looked up).
Travel times are missing or wrong Settings → Travel: check the home address, the transport and Look up times again. Addresses need a street, number and city to be found.
The plan is always too full Settings → My day: lower Plan at most, narrow the suggestion hours, add breathing room after tasks.
A routine takes longer on some days Change the length on a block of that day and choose Every …; or turn on Different length on some days in the routine. With Smart learning on, the app offers it by itself.
I can't put anything in an event's place Events you made in Google stay put: move them in Google Calendar. Invitations can be taken with Not going.
An event is on the wrong day Tap it in the Planner and change the date: its block and all its tied tasks move with it. On the Day view you can also drag the block.
I want my data Settings → Advanced → Your data → Download backup (JSON) gives one file with everything; your repository on GitHub holds the same files.
